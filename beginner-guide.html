Iron Nest Beginner Guide

Everything you need to know before you start playing Iron Nest: Heavy Turret Simulator - what the game is, how the loop works, and the tips that stop your first shells from landing in an empty field.

What Is Iron Nest: Heavy Turret Simulator?

Iron Nest is a first-person dieselpunk artillery simulator. You are the sole operator of a massive, moving fortress armed with heavy guns. There is no aiming reticle and no free camera - you sit inside a steel cabin and turn coordinates into a firing solution using physical instruments: radio intel, a paper map, a ballistic calculator and hand-cranked gunlaying.

The game is a single-player procedure simulator: every shot follows the same deliberate chain, and breaking any link means the shell lands somewhere embarrassing.

The Core Gameplay Loop

  1. Receive orders from High Command and frontline spotter reports via two teleprinters.
  2. Measure the target on the tactical map using bearings and triangulation.
  3. Solve the ballistics on the ballistic calculator to get elevation and charge.
  4. Load the shell and powder charges by hand.
  5. Lay the guns: rotate the turret to bearing, raise the barrel to elevation.
  6. Fire, then correct: watch the impact and adjust the next shot.

11 Tips for New Operators

  1. Keep the clipboard visible (Caps Lock) - key information always in front of you.
  2. Use the clipboard as memory: click any line in the brief to copy it, right-click to delete.
  3. Mark known info in order: Iron Nest position first, then spotters, then reference points.
  4. Focus one target at a time - complete its full loop before moving on.
  5. Enemy artillery is the exception: locate and mark all enemy guns BEFORE firing, because your first round starts the counter-battery timer.
  6. Only keep the bearing and range from the Iron Nest to the target; delete the rest.
  7. Use turret rotation time to prepare elevation or other tasks - swinging thousands of tons takes time.
  8. Be gentle with rotation and elevation levers - rough handling damages machinery.
  9. Never touch the fine wheel while the barrel is moving - it jams the mechanism.
  10. Plan your requisition credits: prioritise ammo, use support actions when needed.
  11. Read both teleprinters before touching anything - frontline chatter often corrects High Command.
