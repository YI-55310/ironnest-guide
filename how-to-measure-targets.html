How to Measure Targets in Iron Nest

The tactical map is the most challenging part of Iron Nest. Learn the grid, the markers and the triangulation method to pin down any target.

The Map Grid System

  • The map is a 20 x 10 grid: 20 columns labelled A-T (left to right) and 10 rows numbered 1-10 (bottom row is 1).
  • Each cell is subdivided into a 10 x 10 sub-grid, labelled starting from 0 - for example 0:4, 9:0.
  • Spotters and known points are given as cell + sub-grid coordinates, e.g. F10 8:0, I7 2:4.

Map Markers (Tokens)

MarkerPurpose
Iron Nest tokenYour own position
Blue numbered markers (1-4)Spotter / recon assets
Green letter markers (A-C)Reference points
Red numbered markers (1-8)Target positions
Red skullConfirmed target destroyed
Blue skullConfirmed friendly-fire casualty

Measurement Steps

1

Mark the Known Information (In Order)

Place the Iron Nest token at your position, then each spotter (e.g. "Spotter 1 - N5 4:0"), then any reference points.

2

Collect the Available Data

You get two kinds of data: bearing (direction from a spotter to the target) and distance (range from a spotter). Typical intel looks like this:

Target #1
  Bearing 186° from Spotter#1
  Bearing 111° from Spotter#2

Target #2
  Distance 7.45km from Spotter#3
  Distance 3.56km from Spotter#1

Sometimes intel is descriptive ("across the river", "near the structure") - estimate and verify by trial.

3

Triangulate With the Clipboard Tools

Your clipboard has red, yellow and white pencils plus a drawing compass.

  1. From Spotter #1's sub-grid cell, draw a line at 186° - draw it long, the intersection is what matters.
  2. From Spotter #2's sub-grid cell, draw a line at 111°.
  3. The intersection of the two lines is the target - place the red target marker.
  4. Finally, draw a straight line from the Iron Nest's cell to the target's cell - the two numbers shown (bearing and range) are auto-saved to your clipboard.

Key Rules

  • Only keep the bearing and range from the Iron Nest to the target. Delete all other auto-added values to avoid confusion.
  • One target at a time - unless the target is enemy artillery, in which case locate and mark ALL enemy guns before firing (the counter-battery timer starts after your first round).
  • The yellow marker measures bearing and range; the red marker records the measurement onto your clipboard for the firing solution. Mixing them up feeds bad numbers into the calculator.
