Iron Nest Elevation & Bearing Guide

Dragging the heavy lever and feeling the whole structure shudder is the most tactile part of Iron Nest. Here is how to lay the guns precisely - and how to do it without breaking the machine.

Set the Turret Rotation (Bearing) First

Always set the rotation before the elevation - swinging the turret takes time, especially for large adjustments, so start it early.

  1. Use the heavy lever to start the turret rotating in the right direction.
  2. As the rotation counter approaches the target value, ease the lever back to center slowly and smoothly to stop the swing.
  3. Use the fine wheel to dial in the exact value until the turret rotation counter matches the bearing on your fire mission card.
Avoid Sudden Movements
Sudden or violent operation can damage the machinery - you'll need to repair it (usually by turning several valves). A machine like the Iron Nest doesn't spin on a whim.

Set the Gun Elevation

The elevation console sits next to the left gun, with two full control sets (one per gun). Early missions usually use only the left gun.

  1. Move the lever to roughly the required elevation and wait for the barrel to arrive. The elevation console stays locked until loading is complete - elevation starts at 0 for loading and resets to 0 after every shot.
  2. Once the barrel has stopped, use the fine wheel to adjust elevation precisely to the value on the fire mission card.
Never Touch the Fine Wheel While the Barrel Is Moving
Interfering mid-movement jams the mechanism and forces a repair before you can continue.

Check Before You Fire

Before pulling the trigger, confirm on the firing console:

  • Elevation matches the card
  • Flight time and loaded shell type are correct
  • Armed status (left gun / right gun / both)
  • Turret rotation matches the bearing

Efficiency Tips

  • Use waiting time well: while the turret rotates, set the elevation or prep the next round. The coffee machine on your desk is there for a reason.
  • Check the elevation gauge after fine-tuning instead of judging the barrel's visual angle - the displayed number is the value tied to your ballistic solution.
  • Change one setting at a time when correcting - altering range, bearing, elevation and charges all at once removes the feedback you need to walk the round onto the target.
