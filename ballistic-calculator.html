Iron Nest Ballistic Calculator Guide

The ballistic calculator turns your map measurements into a firing solution. Here is what every input does and how to read the fire mission card it prints.

What the Calculator Does

The ballistic calculator is the brain of the operation. You feed it the target information gathered from the tactical map, and it computes the exact elevation and powder charge needed to put a shell on target. Skip it and you have no elevation; guess and you'll be correcting for the next ten minutes.

Step-by-Step: Computing a Firing Solution

  1. Enter the distance to target. Rotate the wheel until the counter matches the range value on your clipboard.
  2. Enter the bearing to target. Use the large wheel for coarse adjustment and the small wheel to fine-tune until it matches your clipboard bearing exactly.
  3. Select the shell type. Sometimes High Command specifies it; otherwise judge by target and mission - see Ammo Types.
  4. Select the powder charge. Choose the number of charges based on distance - more charge sends shells farther and flatter, less charge arcs them steeply into entrenched positions.
  5. Select the target type. Infantry, Recon, Artillery, Mechanized, Fire Director or Supply Cache.
  6. Pull the compute lever. The machine prints a fire mission card attached to your clipboard.

Reading the Fire Mission Card

Card PositionValueUsed For
Bottom-leftElevationSetting the gun elevation

Powder Charge Fundamentals

  • More charges = longer range, flatter trajectory, shorter flight time. Useful for fast shots on time-sensitive targets.
  • Fewer charges = steeper arc, deliberate drops into enemy emplacements.
  • Charges cannot be returned once dispensed - make sure you dispense exactly what the card says.
  • Shell flight time is long enough that impatience is genuinely punished. Trust the math.
Pro Tip
The only bearing and range values you need are the ones from the Iron Nest to the target. Delete the rest of the auto-recorded values to keep the clipboard clean.
