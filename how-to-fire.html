How to Fire the Turret in Iron Nest

Operating a 5,000-ton artillery spider should require a training course - and thankfully this game has one very large machine waiting for you. Here is the full process from target to boom, step by step.

The Complete Firing Chain (6 Steps)

Every shot in Iron Nest follows the same sequence. Break one link and the shell lands somewhere embarrassing.

1

Receive Your Orders

Two teleprinters feed you information. One delivers top-priority directives from High Command (targets, ammo type, your position, map data). The other carries frontline traffic and spotter reports with target details: type, bearing and distance.

Tip: Read both printers before touching anything - frontline chatter frequently corrects High Command.

2

Measure the Target

On the tactical map, use the clipboard tools to determine the target's bearing and range. Plot your turret position, place spotter markers, draw bearing lines and find where they intersect - that intersection is your target. Full method: How to Measure Targets.

3

Solve the Ballistics

Enter the target distance and bearing into the ballistic calculator, select the appropriate shell type and powder charge, then pull the compute lever. It prints a fire mission card with the required elevation and turret rotation. Full method: Ballistic Calculator Guide.

4

Load the Gun

From left to right on the loading platform: select the shell with the shell selector lever, ram it into the barrel, dispense the exact number of powder charges shown by the calculator (each of the six levers is one charge), then ram the powder home and close the breech.

One-Way Process
Powder charges cannot be returned once dispensed, and a shell cannot be unloaded once rammed. Each rotary magazine holds six rounds - check your stock before committing.
5

Lay the Guns

Set the bearing first: use the turret rotation mechanism with the fast lever for large movements and the fine wheel for precision. Then set the elevation to match the calculator's value. The elevation console stays locked until loading is complete.

Tip: Swinging that much iron takes time - use the window to prep the next round or make coffee (a genuine mechanic).

Full method: Elevation & Bearing Guide.

6

Fire and Confirm

At the firing console, confirm elevation, flight time, loaded shell type, armed status and turret rotation. Arm the gun with the arming lever (left gun, right gun, or both), then pull the main trigger. Watch the map to see where the round lands - that impact point is your feedback for the next mission.

Firing Tips

  • Plan your shots before firing, especially when enemy artillery is present - counter-battery fire begins after your first round impacts.
  • Use the correct map marker: the yellow marker measures bearing and range, the red marker records the measurement on your clipboard for the firing solution.
  • Check the impact after every shot and make small adjustments to the next firing solution.
  • Most missions want a ranging shell first - aerial recon photos show impact relative to target, then you apply corrections before the main salvo.
  • Adding more powder lowers the required elevation and reduces flight time - useful for a faster shot on a time-sensitive target.
